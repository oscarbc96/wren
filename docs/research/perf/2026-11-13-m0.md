# M0 RAM benchmark (2026-11-13)

Workload `tabs10` (crates/parity/workloads/tabs10.txt), fresh profile, 30 s
after launch, Apple Silicon, 16 GB. Wren is the release build with one
content process per tab (spec 7.2).

| Browser | Metric | Value | Processes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wren | sum of `phys_footprint` | 830 MB | 11 (browser + 10 tabs) |
| Chrome | sum of `phys_footprint` | 815 MB | 20 |
| Wren | sum of RSS (`parity ram`, median of 10) | 1339 MB | 11 |
| Chrome | sum of RSS (all processes of the profile) | 2618 MB | 20 |

Wren breakdown (RSS): browser process 223 MB; tab processes 56 to 174 MB
each, about 1060 MB together.

## Reading

- `phys_footprint` (what Activity Monitor shows as Memory) is the fair
  metric: RSS counts shared pages (the 144 MB binary, system libraries)
  once per process, which inflates every multiprocess browser.
- On footprint Wren is at parity with Chrome, not at the spec 11 target
  (at most 50% of Chrome). The per-tab process cost is the main lever:
  M1 tab sleep (idle tabs exit their process) and the memory budget are
  where the target is won or lost; a per-site process fallback is the
  next step if not (spec 7.2).
- The earlier Chrome figure (462,733,312 bytes RSS,
  `2026-11-13-chrome-tabs10.json`) undercounts Chrome: it was sampled
  before all helper processes were attributed to the root. `parity ram`
  should switch to summing `phys_footprint` over the profile's processes
  (follow-up in M1 Task 9).
